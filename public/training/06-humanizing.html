Humanizing Questions, Labels & QBS

Humanizing Questions

What: Humanizing questions are those that soften the tone of your asks, making them feel less like an interrogation and more like a friendly conversation. Phrases like "Just curious..." or "I don't mean to pry, but..." show that you want to understand rather than grill the prospect. These questions help create a more comfortable atmosphere for discussion.

Why: The value of humanizing questions lies in their ability to build trust and openness. When you approach questions gently, prospects are more likely to feel at ease and share their thoughts. On the other hand, if you come across as aggressive or bombard them with pointed questions, they may become defensive and shut down. Humanized questions encourage a more genuine exchange, which is crucial for building rapport.

How: To effectively use humanizing questions, focus on expressing real curiosity. Start your questions with phrases that invite conversation rather than pressure. For example, saying, "This may sound silly, but..." can help lower defenses and encourage the prospect to open up. By using this approach in your sales conversations, you create a relaxed environment that leads to deeper insights and a more productive dialogue.

Humanizing Phrases

  • "For survey purposes..."
  • "Just curious..."
  • "This may be a dumb question, so go easy on me, but..."
  • "For research purposes..."
  • "I don't mean to pry, but..."

Labels

What: Labeling is a communication strategy that involves making positive observations or comments about a prospect to encourage them to behave in a specific way. By labeling a prospect with desirable traits, you align their self-perception with the actions you want them to take. This technique is particularly effective in overcoming skepticism or hesitation, especially in contexts like discussing impact window solutions.

For example, imagine you are at a restaurant and you want to ask a waitress for a complimentary extra scoop of ice cream. Before making your request, you might say something like, "You are such a nice and attentive waitress; I really appreciate your service!" By labeling her as "nice," you create a positive atmosphere and increase the likelihood that she will respond favorably to your request.

Why

  • Building Rapport: Labeling helps establish a connection between you and the prospect. When a person feels recognized and valued, they are more likely to be open to your suggestions.
  • Influencing Behavior: By labeling someone with a positive trait, you subtly encourage them to embody that trait. For instance, if the waitress feels recognized as "attentive," she may be more inclined to go the extra mile to make your dining experience enjoyable, including considering your request for extra ice cream.
  • Addressing Skepticism: In the context of impact windows, homeowners may have doubts or hesitations. By labeling them positively, such as saying, "You seem like someone who really cares about making smart decisions for your home," you can create a more receptive environment for discussing the benefits of window solutions. This is also referred to as verbalizing the inner dialogue.

Question Based Selling

In the competitive world of sales, the ability to navigate conversations effectively can be the difference between success and failure. One of the most powerful tools at a salesperson's disposal is the art of question-based selling. This method empowers sales professionals to take control of the sales conversation, guiding prospects toward a solution that meets their needs.

What is Question-Based Selling? Question-based selling is a sales technique that emphasizes the importance of asking strategic questions throughout the sales process. Unlike traditional selling methods that focus on presenting products or solutions upfront, question-based selling prioritizes understanding the prospect's needs, pain points, and motivations. By engaging in a dialogue driven by questions, salespeople can foster a more collaborative and receptive environment.

Why Use Question-Based Selling?

  • Control of the Conversation: The adage "he who asks questions controls the conversation" holds true in sales. When you ask questions, you guide the dialogue, allowing you to uncover valuable information about the prospect's needs and concerns.
  • Building Rapport: Questions help to establish familiarity and trust between the salesperson and the prospect. By engaging with thoughtful inquiries, you demonstrate genuine interest in their situation, which can lead to a stronger relationship.
  • Identifying Pain Points: Instead of making assumptions, asking questions enables prospects to articulate their challenges. This insight allows you to tailor your presentation to address their specific issues effectively.
  • Lowering Sales Resistance: When prospects respond to questions, they become active participants in the conversation. This reduces their defensiveness and opens them up to considering your solutions.
  • Enhancing Engagement: Questions create a dynamic interaction, keeping the prospect engaged and involved in the discussion. This active participation increases the likelihood of a positive outcome.

How to Implement Question-Based Selling

Step 1
Start with Engaging Questions

Begin your interaction with questions that establish rapport and engage the prospect.

Examples

"Is this the Martinez residence?" — "Did your neighbors mention that I would be stopping by?"

Step 2
Involve the Prospect in the Discussion

As you transition into your presentation, ask questions that relate to the prospect's experiences.

Examples

"Have you seen the construction on the windows in the area?" — "You know who they are making pay for it, right?"

Step 3
Identify Pain Points

To uncover the prospect's pain points, ask targeted questions that encourage them to reflect on their situation.

Examples

"Have you noticed a small increase in your insurance premiums recently, or a large one?" — "Are you on Citizens or another carrier?"

Step 4
Isolating Objections Through Questions

Once you have established the prospect's pain points, guide the conversation toward potential solutions without making direct statements. Instead, ask.

Examples

"Apart from the cost, was there anything else that held you back or was that pretty much it?" — "If we could guarantee full impact rating and no premium hikes, would that make sense for you?"

Step 5
Address Objections with Curiosity

When objections arise, respond with curiosity rather than defensiveness.

Examples

"Just curious, what specifically held you back in the past?" — "I don't mean to pry, but what exactly were you hoping to get out of the last time you checked into impact windows?"

Step 6
Confirm Understanding and Close

Finally, confirm that you have addressed all the prospect's concerns before moving forward.

Examples

"Was that the only reason you hesitated?" — "Is there anything else that would prevent you from making a decision today?"

Question-based selling is a powerful technique that enables sales professionals to take control of conversations, build rapport, and uncover the needs of their prospects. By prioritizing questions over statements, you can lower sales resistance and create a more engaging and productive dialogue. Embrace these principles, practice them diligently, and watch as you enhance your sales performance and achieve greater success in your career. Take control of the sale, help your customers find solutions, and reap the rewards of effective question-based selling.
