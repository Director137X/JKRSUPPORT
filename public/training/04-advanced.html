Advanced Training: Handling Resistance

Handling Resistance in Sales: Embracing the Challenge

Resistance is not just a hurdle in the world of door-to-door sales; it's the very essence of the game. If homeowners didn't have concerns or objections, our roles would be obsolete. The truth is, the reason we earn our keep in this field is our ability to navigate and conquer customer resistance. Just like a world champion boxer facing an ordinary challenger, we possess the skills and confidence to tackle any objection. So, when you knock on that door, approach it with the mindset of a seasoned professional-you can't lose.

The ADSDI Framework: A Strategic Approach

When you encounter pushback after the initial pitch, leverage the ADSDI framework: Agree, Differentiate, Specialize, Dismiss, and Isolate. Each step provides a structured method to address concerns while reinforcing your authority as a knowledgeable expert.

1. Agree: Begin by validating the customer's feelings. Acknowledging their concerns fosters trust and sets a collaborative tone for the conversation.

2. Differentiate: Differentiate yourself from the typical sales pitch. Emphasize that you understand their unique situation and that not all solutions apply to everyone.

3. Specialize: This is where you showcase your expertise. Let the customer know that you specialize in addressing their specific objection or concern. By doing this, you reassure them that they're not just another sale; you're there to cater to their unique needs.

4. Dismiss: Dismissing their objection as a part of the process normalizes their concerns. You can say something like, "I completely understand why you'd feel that way; it's a common concern." This allows you to sidestep their resistance while positioning yourself as an ally.

5. Isolate: Finally, ask if there's anything else holding them back. This step isolates the objection, allowing you to address any remaining concerns while keeping the conversation focused.

Techniques to Tackle Resistance

Feel-Felt-Found: The Power of Storytelling

The Feel-Felt-Found method is a classic storytelling technique that helps you connect with the customer on a personal level. It allows you to share relatable experiences from past customers, which diminishes the bias that often comes with sales pitches.

1. Feel: Start by acknowledging the prospect's feelings and concerns.

2. Felt: Share that others have felt the same way, establishing a sense of community in their experience.

3. Found: Conclude with how those concerns were resolved and the positive outcomes that followed.

Example

Objection: "A lot of my friends got impact windows, and they didn't save money. They ended up with a big premium from Citizens!"

Response: "I completely understand your concern. Many of my previous customers felt the same way, worried about ending up with a high premium. But what they found is that by getting full impact rating on their system, like your neighbor Kevin, they rarely worry about storms. Now, he pays for his insurance at a lower rate and hardly pays anything to Citizens!"

NSD Technique: Tailoring Solutions

The NSD (Normalize, Specialize, Dismiss) technique allows you to cater a solution to the customer's specific objection, reinforcing your role as an expert.

1. Normalize: Reassure the customer that their objection is common.

2. Specialize: Communicate that you specialize in addressing this particular concern.

3. Dismiss: Explain that you only work with customers who share this specific situation, which emphasizes your expertise and builds value.

Example

Objection: "I don't want to proceed because my stucco/siding is old; I'd rather replace it first."

Response: "Absolutely, that's a common concern around here. I specialize in working with older exteriors, so it shouldn't be an issue at all. In fact, I only work with homes with older exteriors; if yours were brand new, I wouldn't even be able to help you!"

Conclusion: Embrace Resistance as a Path to Success

Resistance in sales is not merely an obstacle; it's an opportunity for connection and growth. By adopting the ADSDI framework and utilizing storytelling techniques like Feel-Felt-Found and NSD, you can effectively navigate objections and solidify your role as a trusted advisor. Remember, every objection is a chance to build rapport and demonstrate your expertise. Embrace the challenge, approach each conversation with confidence, and watch as resistance transforms into acceptance, paving the way for successful sales.

Smokescreens

Within a sales process there is always somebody that gets closed within the process, it will either be you closing the prospect on that they will need your product, or it will be the prospect closing you on that they do not need your product.

What is a Smokescreen Objection?

Smokescreens are not valid objections, they are things to steer you off of your path. An objection is an actual concern that a customer needs help resolving, smoke screens need absolutely no energy to them. So MAKE SURE YOU DON'T put any time into them.

The Biggest problem a first year rep will deal with is actually believing smokescreens and thinking they are actually valid. If you knock on someone's door and they answer it in the middle of the day, I can promise you that person is not actually busy. DO NOT FALL into the trap of believing smokescreens. Remember, you are there to sell them, make sure that you don't get sold by them!

We Use AIM on Overcoming Smokescreens

AIM (Acknowledge, Ignore, Move on)

  • Acknowledge: this is a way for you to neutralize the situation when we use our acknowledged words: (yeah, for sure, totally, Of course,) we need to make sure that we use them in an up tone.
  • Ignore: this is a way for you to make sure that you have complete confidence while you move on. You can not show within your body language that you just blew by their objection. If you show within your facial expressions or body language they will instantly know that you don't actually mean business.
  • Move On: This is the part where you have to get right back on your path of your pitch, if you were in question + movement, but got a smokescreen right before you have to be able to AIM, and move on right to the point of your sales process you were in.

These will most commonly pop up within these parts of the roadmap: breaking preoccupation, questions + movement, and justifying existence.

Most Common Smokescreens

  • "I'm busy right now"
  • "I'm on a call right now"
  • "Can I get a card"
  • "Have to talk to my partner"
  • "I'm good"
  • "Not interested"
  • "You're, like, the 5th guy to come by"
  • "Not today"
  • "Can you come later"

Verbalize Inner Dialogue

Verbalizing the inner dialogue of a prospect involves articulating what you intuitively sense they are thinking or feeling during a sales conversation. It's about acknowledging their unspoken concerns, hesitations, or emotions by using phrases like, "It seems like you might be feeling..." or "Sounds like you're saying...". This technique allows you to express empathy and understanding, creating a more genuine connection with the prospect.

Why is This Important?

Verbalizing a prospect's inner dialogue is crucial because it helps build trust. When you accurately reflect what someone may be thinking, you demonstrate that you are not only listening but also attuned to their emotional state. This level of awareness fosters an environment where the prospect feels seen and understood, making them more likely to open up and engage in the conversation. Trust is a fundamental component of any successful sales interaction; when prospects feel comfortable, they are more inclined to share their concerns or objections, paving the way for a more productive discussion.

How to Effectively Verbalize Inner Dialogue

To effectively verbalize a prospect's inner dialogue, you can use specific word tracks that resonate with their feelings. Here are a couple of examples:

Word Tracks

"It sounds like you're saying the biggest concern is that you are worried about getting double billed?"

"It seems like you may believe this sounds too good to be true."

By incorporating these phrases into your conversations, you not only validate the prospect's feelings but also create an opportunity for deeper dialogue. This approach allows you to address any underlying concerns they may have, guiding the conversation toward resolution and ultimately progressing through the sales roadmap.

In summary, verbalizing the inner dialogue of your prospects is a powerful tool in the sales process. By acknowledging their unspoken thoughts and feelings, you build trust and create an atmosphere conducive to open communication. This practice not only enhances your connection with the prospect but also equips you with the insights needed to guide them effectively through their decision-making journey.
